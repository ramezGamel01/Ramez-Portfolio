Rihla Egypt by bus, in 3D.

A bus booking app for travel around Egypt, rebuilt in React with three.js: a 3D desert on the home page, a journey you drive by scrolling, and seats you can pick inside a 3D coach. In English and Arabic.

The problem

Booking sites tend to look the same: a form, a list, a grid of seats. The first Rihla showed the whole flow could work in plain HTML and CSS. The rebuild asks what a booking app feels like when it's also somewhere you want to look around.

So the search stays quick and practical, and around it the country is in 3D: the desert on the home page, the route across a map of Egypt, and the inside of the coach when you choose a seat.

What it does

  • A 3D hero: a coach crosses the desert past the pyramids at sunset, or under the stars in the dark theme. In Arabic the scene mirrors and the coach drives right to left.
  • A scroll-driven journey across a 3D map of Egypt, from Alexandria to Hurghada, with the route drawn behind the coach.
  • Seats on a 2D map or inside a 3D cabin you can turn and click, both showing the same selection.
  • Search with a date strip of the cheapest fare per day, filters for price, time, class, operator and amenities, and four sort orders.
  • Checkout with passenger validation, a ten-minute seat hold, a promo code and three ways to pay. It's a demo, so no payment is taken.
  • A flippable ticket with a QR code, calendar export and print, and a bookings page where cancelling releases the seats.

How it's built

React 19 and JavaScript with React Router and Vite, each page loaded on demand. The 3D scenes use three.js through React Three Fiber and live in their own chunk that loads after the page is on screen. Trips come from a seeded timetable, so a trip always has the same departure, price and taken seats; bookings and the demo account live in the browser.

Decisions that shaped it

3D that loads second

three.js is split into its own chunk and fetched only once the page is showing, so the search works before any 3D arrives. Without WebGL, the page keeps its normal layout.

Scrolling drives the camera

The journey section is five screens tall with a sticky map. The scroll position becomes a point along the route; the coach moves there, the trail draws up to it and the camera eases after it.

One selection, two views

The 2D grid and the 3D cabin read and write the same list of seats and the same limit. The grid is the accessible one: a single tab stop, with arrow keys to move between seats.

Arabic as a first language

Every string has an Arabic version with proper plural forms, numbers use Arabic-Indic digits, the layout flips right to left, and the hero scene mirrors so the coach drives right to left.

The same data every time

Trips come from a seeded random generator keyed on route and date, so links to results, trips and checkout keep working after a reload, and tests can check exact results.
